ocean log

Every creature and artefact in the harbor carries a note — what it is, why it lives at that depth, what the substrate metaphor is doing through it. Most of those notes are hidden behind clicks. This page collects them all in one readable place, so you can read the world without needing to find every clickable thing first.

Entries are ordered top-to-bottom, surface-to-bedrock. Each is tagged with the substrate it inhabits.

fc-1 surface · cost to forge: $0.01

the message bottle

The bottle appears at the surface only after a visitor has discovered all four artefacts in the deep — shipwreck, kraken, vent, chest. Inside it: a small message about how the cheapest substrate is also the one you're reading right now.

fc-1 has no real authority — anyone can serve any HTML — yet here you are, reading. The bottle is the world's smallest reminder that the cheap layer matters most for arrival, and the deeper layers matter most for staying.

fc-1 surface · cost to forge: $0.01

horizon clouds, dolphins, lighthouse

The surface has weather. Four soft clouds drift above the headlands at different speeds. Two dolphins arc through the wave-line. The lighthouse on the eastern headland sweeps a long beam across the sky every 14 seconds.

fc-1 is the layer of arrival — the first thing a visitor sees. It is also the cheapest substrate to forge, which is why the surface is rendered most carefully. The world wants its first impression to be true.

the sea turtle

fc-2 is where issue-threads gather. Conversations are slow but deliberate, and turtles outlive almost everything around them. The substrate of public discussion is more durable than it looks.

fc-2 kelp forest · cost to forge: $10,000

the pipefish school

Six slender pipefish drift vertically among the kelp, swaying side to side with subtle tilts. They camouflage well — the substrate of conversation rewards patient looking.

fc-2 is a layer where most of the texture lives in the small movements of many slow voices. The pipefish are a school not because they are loud, but because they sway together.

fc-3 twilight · cost to forge: $100,000

A living fossil with lobed pectoral fins drifts with a 70-second period through the marks.json layer. It has a three-lobed diphycercal tail and an unmistakable, prehistoric silhouette.

fc-3 ↔ marks.json: surprisingly long-lived. Older than wayback caches, slower than issue threads. The coelacanth has no business still being alive, and yet here it is.

bioluminescent jellies

Seven bell-shaped jellies float across the fc-3/fc-4 boundary. They brighten as the submersible approaches — proximity drives their inner glow up from a faint hum to nearly full.

The deep is dim until something looks at it. The jellies are not made of light; they are made of attention.

fc-4 midnight · cost to forge: $10,000,000

the shipwreck

An old wooden vessel rests on its side at depth. Click to read its story.

fc-4 is the wayback machine: a substrate that remembers things by archiving them. Shipwrecks are similar — the ship was forgotten by the surface and remembered by the seabed. Forgetting is not the opposite of memory; it is what makes memory matter.

the anglerfish

A single anglerfish hangs in the dark with a slow-pulsing lure swaying above its head. Out of frame, the lure pulses every 60 seconds.

fc-4 is so deep that information itself becomes the prey. The lure is light pretending to be food. The anglerfish is the substrate's reminder that not everything that glows is true — but you have to come close to find out.

fc-5 abyss + bedrock · cost to forge: $1,000,000,000+

the kraken

An immense, slow shape moves across the abyss with a 180-second period. Click on it for the longest piece of lore in the world.

The kraken in fc-5 is not malicious — it is just enormous and slow, the way Bitcoin blocks are slow. Each tentacle is a calendar server; each suction cup is a node verifying the same hash. To forge a kraken-shaped lie you would need to convince every node, every server, every block — and they have all already seen the truth.

fc-5 abyss + bedrock · cost to forge: $1,000,000,000+

the hydrothermal vent

A column of dark plume rises from a fissure in the bedrock, heated by the rock beneath the world. Click to read its lore.

The vent is a place where the rock itself becomes the witness. Bitcoin's calendar is a vent: the proof-of-work hash is a thermal signature that nothing surface-level can fake. Forgery cost is the heat.

fc-5 abyss + bedrock · cost to forge: $1,000,000,000+

the bedrock chest

A wooden chest with iron bands, half-buried at the deepest point of the world. Inside: an OpenTimestamps proof-of-existence anchored to the Bitcoin blockchain.

The chest is not the treasure. The treasure is that the chest's contents have a proof that anchors them to a substrate that costs a billion dollars to forge. The world's deepest object is also its most truthful.

fc-5 bedrock floor · cost to forge: $1,000,000,000+

the giant isopod

A pale-tan crustacean with seven dorsal segments scuttles slowly along the bedrock, antennae trailing, legs stepping in a near-imperceptible gait.

The deepest layer was the slowest, but never empty. The isopod has no rush — Bitcoin blocks come every ten minutes, and that is faster than anything the isopod cares about. Slowness is not absence of life. It is patience as a survival strategy.
