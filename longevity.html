what longevity adds to forgery cost

A receipt timestamped one minute ago is technically on the substrate, but almost no one outside The Anchorage has cached it yet. A receipt timestamped three months ago has been crawled by search engines, mirrored by archives, quoted in linked pages, perhaps printed. Silently rewriting that receipt now requires colluding with every mirror.

This is why oldest matters more than count. One ancient mark on a given substrate is harder to forge than a hundred fresh ones — because the forger has to undo not just the bytes but the propagation.

Substrate 4 (Wayback) is the one substrate where The Anchorage itself cannot easily add new old marks; the Internet Archive timestamps independently. Substrate 5 (OpenTimestamps / Bitcoin) anchors into a substrate where the cost of inserting a backdated entry is the cost of mining a longer fork — measured today in billions.
